# PRD Quality Review — Todo App

## Overall verdict
The PRD has a real thesis ("a packed day and an ordinary morning use the same to-do list"), a disciplined glossary, honest non-goals, and two concrete journeys. Scope decisions like "alarm is shown and never fired" and "no authentication" are stated as decisions, not hedged. The weak point is the core of UJ-2. The PRD never says how the product decides "what is next" when nothing is marked done. It also leaves Today's ordering, "high-priority", copy semantics, and data persistence under no-auth undefined. For a product that has already shipped, someone maintaining it can't tell from the PRD what the app actually does in exactly the places that matter most. Verdict: adequate frame, thin core.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Most v1 choices are stated flatly and closed. §5 Non-Goals and §6.2 remove authentication, calendar, native apps, and auto-fill without hedging. §6.2 also records two former open questions as "decided and shipped": No date placement, and an alarm that is never fired. §8 has one genuinely open, low-stakes question, with an owner and a trigger. This is the opposite of a PRD that "balances" everything.

The gap is that trade-offs are named by what was chosen, not by what was given up. Removing sign-in is the clearest case. The PRD says "Tom is not asked to identify himself" (§5). It never says what that costs for a product he uses at both phone width and desktop width (§Platform). One possibility is that his to-dos don't follow him across devices. The other is that anyone who reaches the site sees and edits them. A reviewer pushing back on "no auth" would find the objection dodged, not acknowledged.

### Findings
- **high** No-auth consequence for data ownership and persistence is unstated (§5 "v1 has no authentication and no sign-in"; §Platform "same capabilities are usable at phone width and at desktop width"; UJ-1 Entry state "He is not asked to sign in") — The shipped product must do one of three things: store data per browser, store it on a shared open backend, or rely on some unstated access control. Each has a different failure mode: lost or unsynced lists, or exposed and editable lists. The PRD gives no signal. *Fix:* Add one sentence under §Platform or §5 saying where to-dos are stored and who can reach them in the shipped v1. Name the accepted cost (for example, "lists do not sync between phone and desktop" or "anyone with the URL can see and edit").
- **medium** Landing screen is explicitly left unstated (UJ-1 Entry state: "Which to-do list or day he lands on is unstated.") — For a shipped product this is a known fact, not an open decision. UJ-2 says "The first thing he opens is Today", which suggests Today is either the landing screen or one tap away. *Fix:* State what the shipped app opens to.

## Substance over theater — strong
There's one persona, and he drives every decision. The non-users (§2.2) are there because FR-16 depends on them. The Vision (§1) can't be swapped into another to-do PRD: it names the Saturday English/ballet/Costco day, the "copied … and confirmed" rule, and the all-time not-done view. There's no innovation section and no boilerplate NFRs. The counter-metric SM-C1 is earned: it names a specific way the product could go wrong ("the weekday habit is being pushed onto the packed day").

### Findings
- **low** Success Metrics are acceptance checks restated (§7 SM-1, SM-2: "Target: he can do this.") — They repeat FR consequences and add no measurement. That's acceptable when there's one user and no usage target ("No usage target was supplied"). But calling them metrics overstates them. *Fix:* Either relabel §7 as launch acceptance checks, or state how each is observed (manual walkthrough, at which widths).

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and the features follow from it. The not-done view and period views (FR-3, FR-4) serve "every to-do that is not done remains visible across all time". Partial (FR-17) and "what is next without marking done" (FR-7) serve the packed day. The no-auto-pick and no-auto-fill rules (FR-9, FR-15) keep Tom in control on both days. The MVP is a problem-solving scope and reads as one.

The metrics check that capabilities exist, not that the thesis holds. Nothing checks that the same to-do list actually works for both the weekday morning and the Saturday.

### Findings
- **medium** No metric tests the "same to-do list" thesis, and SM-C1 is unmeasurable as written (§1 "a packed day and an ordinary morning use the same to-do list"; §7 SM-C1 "Saturday mark-done rate") — SM-1 and SM-2 test each journey on its own. SM-C1 has no baseline, no observation method, and no data source. With no auth and one user, it's unclear who would compute a "rate". *Fix:* State how SM-C1 is observed (for example, Tom's own review of a given Saturday), or downgrade it to a design guardrail. Optionally tie SM-1 and SM-2 to the same to-do list so the thesis is checked.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
The FRs are well formed. Every one has testable consequences, and several are sharp: FR-3's three-way inclusion test, FR-8's "rejects a priority outside 1 through 5", and FR-13's "does not send or play anything". But the behaviors that carry the journeys are the least specified. An engineer, tester, or maintainer can't tell what the shipped app does when FR-2 and FR-7 meet UJ-2's real data.

### Findings
- **critical** "What is next" has no defined mechanism (FR-2 "Tom can identify the next to-do from that order"; FR-7 "Today still shows … which one is next"; UJ-2 Climax "the to-do list tells him the next stop") — This is UJ-2's climax, and UJ-2 says Tom "may not mark each to-do done". So "next" can't mean "first not-done to-do". It must come from the clock, from position, or from something else, and the PRD never says which. FR-2's consequence only requires that Tom *can identify* it. FR-7's requires that Today "shows" it. Those are different claims. *Fix:* State the shipped rule in FR-7. For example: "Next is the first to-do in Today's order whose time is at or after the current time; untimed to-dos are next when the preceding timed to-do's time has passed." Also state how it's displayed. Make FR-2's consequence match.
- **high** What Today is, and how it's ordered, is undefined (Glossary "Today — today's to-dos, in order"; FR-2 "in list order"; FR-15 "onto Saturday's Today"; UJ-2 Path step 1) — Today is described as a view of the current date's to-dos (FR-2). It's also described as a destination to-do list (FR-15). Its relationship to the day view for the current date (FR-4) is never stated. "List order" is undefined. UJ-2's stops include untimed to-dos ("go home and rest", "go to Costco with his wife") between timed ones. That means order can't be purely by time, yet no FR covers manual ordering. *Fix:* In the Glossary and FR-2, say whether Today is a to-do list or a date-filtered view, which to-do lists it draws from, and the shipped ordering rule (time, manual position, or creation order).
- **high** "High-priority" has no threshold, and finding high-priority to-dos has no FR (UJ-1 Path step 1 "checks the app for to-dos that are important or high priority"; FR-9 consequence "When more than one high-priority to-do is not done") — FR-8 defines priority as 1–5 but never says which values count as "high". So FR-9's consequence can't be tested. UJ-1 step 1 depends on Tom finding these to-dos, but no FR says whether any view sorts, filters, or highlights by priority. *Fix:* Define "high-priority" in the Glossary (for example, 4 or 5, or "relative to other to-dos"). Add the shipped sort or display behavior to FR-8 or FR-9 as a consequence.
- **high** Copy semantics are under-specified (FR-15 "Tom can copy to-dos from one to-do list onto another, including onto Saturday's Today") — Several things are unstated. Is the copy all-or-selected? Which fields carry over (time, priority, alarm, notes, sub-to-dos, partial or done state, named person)? How do copied to-dos get Saturday's date? What does an unconfirmed copy look like, and can it be cancelled? FR-12's 9:00am/10:00am examples only hold if time carries over. *Fix:* Add consequences to FR-15 covering selection, carried fields, date assignment, and the pending-then-cancel path, as shipped.
- **medium** Sub-to-do behavior is unbounded (FR-10; Glossary "Sub-to-do — a to-do that belongs to another to-do") — The PRD doesn't say whether sub-to-dos have their own done or partial state, priority, and time. It doesn't say whether they appear in the not-done view and period views on their own, or how deep nesting goes. It doesn't say what marking the parent done (FR-11) or deleting it (FR-5) does to them. *Fix:* Add consequences to FR-10 for state, view membership, depth, and parent done/delete behavior.
- **medium** Partial state contradicts itself (Glossary "Partial — the state of a to-do that is neither done nor not done"; Glossary "Not-done view … A partial to-do is included"; FR-17 "distinct from a done to-do and from a to-do he has not started") — The Glossary calls partial "neither … nor not done" but puts it in the not-done view. FR-17 brings in "not started" as a third, undefined state. *Fix:* Define the states as done / not done / partial, with partial counted as not done for view membership. Replace "not started" with "not done".
- **medium** Relationship between alarm and time is unclear (Glossary "Alarm — a time shown on a time-bound to-do"; FR-13 "A to-do with no time is not required to have an alarm") — "Time-bound" isn't defined. It's unclear whether the alarm can differ from the to-do's time. FR-13's last consequence leaves open whether an untimed to-do *can* have an alarm. *Fix:* Define "time-bound" as "has a time". State whether the alarm is its own clock value or a flag on the time, and whether it requires a time.
- **low** Period boundaries and the "current date" are unspecified (FR-2 "the current date's to-dos"; FR-4 "the week … that contain that date") — Week start day and which timezone defines "today" are unstated. These are low-impact for one user in one place, but they're shipped behavior. *Fix:* One line each in FR-4 and FR-2.

## Scope honesty — adequate
The explicit omissions are well handled. §5 and §6.2 cover accounts, auto-pick, platforms, auto-add, auth, calendar, and the "quick to finish" field. The addendum keeps the calendar removal out of the body while warning designers off it. Open-items density is low, which suits shipped stakes: one Open Question and one inline `[ASSUMPTION]`.

What's missing is the set of shipped behaviors the reader has to infer. These aren't new features. They're behaviors the existing product must already have, one way or the other.

### Findings
- **medium** Shipped behaviors left to inference (FR-5 "An update to its name, time, or priority"; UJ-1 Path step 1; FR-2 "list order") — The PRD doesn't say whether a to-do's date can be set or changed (FR-5 lists name, time, and priority only, while the Glossary and FR-4 depend on the date). It doesn't say whether Tom can reorder to-dos, or how to-do lists themselves are created, renamed, or deleted (FR-1 only "sees" them). Each is either shipped or a deliberate gap, and the reader can't tell which. *Fix:* For each one, add a consequence to the relevant FR, or a line in §6.2 if it isn't in v1.
- **low** The Assumptions Index mostly holds confirmed decisions (§9: "Confirmed with the vision", "Confirmed with FR-5 and FR-6", "Confirmed with the shipped app") — Four of five entries aren't assumptions, which hides the one real assumption. *Fix:* Move confirmed items to a short "Decisions" list, or drop them, and keep §9 for the ballet-pickup assumption.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The Glossary is present, opinionated ("'Task' and 'item' are not used"), and mostly respected. FR, UJ, and SM IDs are unique, and cross-references resolve. Both UJs have a named protagonist with context inline. Since the product has shipped, the main downstream readers are whoever maintains or tests it. For them, the drift on load-bearing terms matters more than cosmetics.

### Findings
- **medium** Load-bearing terms drift or go undefined (UJ-2 "stops", "today's list", UJ-2 title "today's list"; FR-17 "not started"; FR-13 / §2.1 "time-bound"; UJ-1 / FR-9 "high-priority"; FR-5 "name") — "Stop" is used as a synonym for to-do in UJ-2 and SM-2. "Today's list" blurs whether Today is a to-do list. "Name" (the to-do's title) isn't in the Glossary, even though it's also the verb used in FR-16 ("name a person"). *Fix:* Replace "stop" and "today's list" with "to-do" and "Today". Add Glossary entries for "time-bound", "high-priority", and the to-do's title field.
- **low** The partial job and the open-work job have no journey (§2.1 "Partial work" has no "Realizes"; "Open work" has no UJ) — FR-17 and FR-3/FR-4 trace only to jobs, not to UJs. That's fine for a capability spec, but it isn't stated. *Fix:* Add "No UJ; capability only" to those job lines.

## Shape fit — adequate
A single-operator consumer web app with two distinct days of use makes UJs the right tool, and two is the right number. The PRD isn't over-formalized.

The gap is the brownfield framing. The product is described as "already shipped" (§8, §6.2), but the document reads as a forward spec. Frontmatter says `status: draft`, there are no references to shipped screens or routes, and only two behaviors are marked as confirmed against the shipped app. A maintainer can't tell which statements were checked against the running product and which are intent.

### Findings
- **medium** Shipped product, but the PRD isn't grounded in shipped behavior (frontmatter `status: draft`; §6.2 "The two former open behaviors are decided and shipped"; §9 "Confirmed with the shipped app" appears once) — Downstream readers can't tell intent from verified behavior. *Fix:* Mark the PRD as describing shipped v1 (status plus one line in §6). Confirm the critical and high findings above against the running app, not against the PRD's own text.
- **low** Negative FR and duplicated non-goals (FR-14 "Out of scope for v1" with consequences; §5 and §6.2 near-verbatim) — FR-14 uses an FR slot for a non-goal. §5 and §6.2 repeat the same seven items. *Fix:* Keep FR-14 as a withdrawn stub or move it to §5. Have §6.2 reference §5 instead of repeating it.

## Mechanical notes
- **ID continuity:** FR-1 to FR-17 are unique and contiguous. FR-17 sits out of order in §4.2, between FR-7 and FR-8. FR-14 is withdrawn but keeps its ID, which is fine. UJ-1 and UJ-2, SM-1 to SM-3, and SM-C1 all resolve. SM cross-references to FRs are all valid.
- **Assumptions roundtrip:** There's one inline `[ASSUMPTION]` (UJ-2 Path step 1, "ballet pickup is at 2:30pm, when the lesson ends"). It's indexed in §9 with different wording ("pickup and the lesson end are the same clock"). FR-12 and SM-2 treat 2:30pm as fact without the tag. The other four §9 entries have no inline counterpart because they're confirmed decisions.
- **Change history in the body:** UJ-2 Edge case "The earlier 8:00am English time is withdrawn" and §6 "Native phone support is removed" are revision notes. They belong in the addendum.
- **Section numbering:** "Platform" is unnumbered between §5 and §6.
- **Working title:** "*Working title — confirm.*" under the H1 duplicates Open Question 1.
- **UJ protagonists:** Both name Tom with context inline. ✓
- **Addendum:** It's consistent with the PRD (calendar removed, alarm shown and never fired). ✓
- **Required sections:** All present for a shipped single-user product. There's no NFR section. That's defensible given the scope, except for the persistence and access point raised under Decision-readiness.
